<div class="q-head"><span class="q-no">No.2025080202000001</span><span class="q-badge q-badge-main">Case · 4 小题</span></div>
<div class="q-material">
<p>Jason Miller is the CIO of The Arthur Greenberg Foundation, which is dedicated to providing support to the creative arts. Historically, the foundation has invested in liquid alternative investments, such as REITs, commodities, ETFs, and hedge funds. The foundation investment committee feels that, given the experience of its asset managers, liquidity is an important characteristic of existing and future potential investments in alternatives. Members of the committee have stated that they currently do not want to lock up capital for more than ten years.</p>
<p>The committee has asked Miller to review investment options for liquid alternative assets. Miller reviews several options with Michael Stern, the alternatives portfolio manager. Stern provides the following list as potential investment candidates:</p>
<p>Private equity: Venture capital Private credit: Senior secured collateralized loans Private real assets: Infrastructure Hedge fund: Market-neutral</p>
<p>Miller mentions to Stern that there are also investment opportunities in the secondary market that should be considered and may be appropriate for the foundation. Stern and Miller plan to present investment opportunities to the committee at their next meeting.</p>
<p>Following the presentation, the investment committee is ready to make several long-term private equity investment commitments. The committee conducts liquidity planning, specifically to ensure adequate capital is available for planned private equity commitments. Miller provides a list of private equity funds for consideration:</p>
<p>Buyout Growth equity Venture capital Real estate Energy</p>
<p>A few members of the committee have expressed concerns regarding the complex and long-term nature of alternative investments and have asked Miller if he has the resources to properly monitor a private equity portfolio. Miller asks Stern to research the key factors that must be considered to effectively monitor private equity managers.</p>
</div>

<p>Bill Ford has just been hired by the Bellevue Hill University Endowment (Bellevue). Ford previously worked as a consultant, advising clients on strategic asset allocations to alternative investments. Bellevue is attempting to further diversify the endowment portfolio, which is currently allocated 60/40 to publicly traded equities and fixed income. The CIO of Bellevue is meeting with Ford to discuss his plan for allocating available capital to alternative investment asset classes.</p>
<p>The CIO begins the conversation by asking Ford about the functional roles that alternative investments can play in a portfolio. Ford provides a brief overview.</p>
<p>Ford then asks the CIO for her expectations of the endowment portfolio’s performance once an allocation is made to alternative investments. The CIO states:</p>
<p>Statement 1: I am excited about the low correlation of returns between the current portfolio and alternative investments and how that will benefit the portfolio when markets enter higher levels of volatility. The lower volatility of alternative investment returns and the resulting correlation with other asset class returns makes alternative investments a superior risk-reducing addition to a portfolio.</p>
<p>The endowment’s head of research, Stephanie McGovern, joins the meeting and begins describing the most recent macroeconomic forecast, which primarily discusses increasing inflation and strong economic growth. The CIO asks Ford the following question:</p>
<p>Question 1: Using a traditional approach to classifying alternative investment asset classes, can you identify which asset classes would be most appropriate for the endowment?</p>
<p>The endowment presently uses a risk-based approach when constructing its publicly traded equity portfolio. Before the meeting concludes, the CIO asks Ford if he has experience using risk-based approaches when making asset allocation decisions. Ford replies that he has expertise in determining the specific embedded risk factors across various alternative investment asset classes.</p>
