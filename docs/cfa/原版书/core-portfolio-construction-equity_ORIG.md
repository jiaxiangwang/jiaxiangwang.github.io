<p>Barboa Fund Invests solely in the equity of companies in oil production and</p>
<p>transportation industries in many countries.</p>
<p>Caribou Fund Uses an aggressive strategy focusing on relatively new, fast-growing</p>
<p>companies in emerging industries.</p>
<p>DoGood Fund Investment universe includes all US companies and sectors that have</p>
<p>favorable environmental, social, and governance (ESG) ratings and</p>

<p>based on the assumption that markets are efficient.</p>
<p>Hans Smith, an Albright portfolio manager, makes the following notes after examining these funds:</p>
<p>Note 1 The fee on the Caribou Fund is a 15% share of any capital appreciation above a 7% threshold and the use of a high-water mark.</p>
<p>Note 2 The DoGood Fund invests in Fleeker Corporation stock, which is rated high in the ESG space, and Fleeker’s pension fund has a significant investment in the DoGood Fund. This dynamic has the potential for a conflict of interest on the part of Fleeker Corporation but not for the DoGood Fund.</p>
<p>Note 3 The DoGood Fund’s portfolio manager has written policies stating that the fund does not engage in shareholder activism. Therefore, the DoGood Fund may be a free-rider on the activism by these shareholders.</p>
<p>Note 4 Of the four funds, the Elmer Fund is most likely to appeal to investors who want to minimize fees and believe that the market is efficient.</p>
<p>Note 5 Adding investment-grade bonds to the Elmer Fund will decrease the portfolio’s short-term risk.</p>
<p>Smith discusses means of enhancing income for the three funds with the junior analyst, Kolton Frey, including engaging in securities lending or writing covered calls. Frey tells Smith the following:</p>
<p>Statement 1 Securities lending would increase income through reinvestment of the cash collateral but would require the fund to miss out on dividend income from the lent securities.</p>
<p>Statement 2 Writing covered calls would generate income, but doing so would limit the upside share price appreciation for the underlying shares.</p>

<p>Evan Winthrop, a senior officer of a US-based corporation, meets with Rebecca Tong, a portfolio manager at Cobalt Wealth Management. Winthrop recently moved his investments to Cobalt in response to his previous manager’s relative underperformance and high expenses.</p>
<p>Winthrop resides in Canada and plans to retire there. His annual salary covers his current spending needs, and his vested defined benefit pension plan is sufficient to meet retirement income goals. Winthrop desires exposure to global equity markets with a focus on low management costs and minimal tracking error to any index benchmarks. The fixed-income portion of the portfolio may consist of laddered maturities with a home-country bias.</p>
<p>Tong proposes using an index-based equity strategy and reviews the most important requirements for an appropriate benchmark. With regard to investable indexes, Tong tells Winthrop the following:</p>
<p>Statement 1 A free-ﬂoat adjustment to a market-capitalization-weighted index lowers its liquidity.</p>
<p>Statement 2 An index provider that incorporates a buﬀering policy makes the index more investable.</p>
<p>Winthrop asks Tong to select a benchmark for the equity allocation that holds all sectors of the Canadian equity market and to focus the portfolio on highly liquid, well-known companies. In addition, Winthrop specifes that any stock purchased should have a relatively low beta, a high dividend yield, a low P/E, and a low price-to-book ratio (P/B).</p>
