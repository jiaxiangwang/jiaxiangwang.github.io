### 2. The investment committee also asks Bookman to investigate whether the endowment

should increase its allocation to illiquid investments to take advantage of higher potential returns. The endowment’s liquidity profile policy stipulates that at least 30% of investments must be classified as liquid to support operating expenses; no more than 40% should be classified as illiquid. Bookman decides to perform a bottom-up liquidity analysis to respond to the committee. Discuss the elements of Bookman’s analysis and the conclusions he will draw from it.

> **答案要点**
>
> • Operationalize liquidity budget concepts by analyzing and monitoring underlying
> liquidity characteristics of portfolio investments over time.
> • Look beyond broad asset class definition to underlying investments for liquidity
> analysis as they can have different profiles within the same class.
> Bookman conducts bottom-up liquidity analysis on State Tech endowment by
> •
> multiplying asset class allocation with matching liquidity classification and aggregating
> across asset classes.
> • Based on the analysis, 44.5% of investments are classified as liquid and 28.5% as
> illiquid.
> Investments classified as liquid = (Cash allocation × %Liquid) + (Fixed-income allocation
> × %Liquid) + (Public equity allocation × %Liquid) = (1% × 100%) + (24% × 100%) + (39% × 50%)
> = 44.5%
> Investments classified as illiquid = (Private equity allocation × %Illiquid) + (Real asset allocation
> × %Illiquid) = (21% × 100%) + (15% × 50%) = 28.5%
> The 44.5% liquid investment allocation is above the 30% requirement, and the 28.5% illiquid
> one is below the 40% limit. There's enough capacity to reallocate funds from liquid to illiquid
> for higher returns. Bookman can recommend this shift.

### 5. In its quarterly policy and performance review, the investment team for the Per-alandra

University endowment identified a tactical allocation opportunity in international developed equities. The team also decided to implement a passive 1% overweight (USD5 million notional value) position in the asset class. Implementation will occur by using either an MISC EAFE Index ETF in the cash market or the equivalent futures contract in the derivatives market. The team determined that the unlevered cost of implementation is 27 bps in the cash market (ETF) and 32 bps in the derivatives market (futures). This modest cost differential prompted a comparison of costs on a levered basis to preserve liquidity for upcoming capital commitments in the fund’s alternative investment asset classes. For the related analysis, the

team’s assumptions are as follows:

✓ Investment policy compliant at three times leverage ✓ Investment horizon of one year ✓ Three-month MRR of 1.8% ✓ ETF borrowing cost of three-month MRR plus 35 bps Recommend the most cost-effective strategy. Justify your response with calculations of the total levered cost of each implementation option.

> **答案要点**
>
> As the lower-cost alternative, the endowment’s investment team should implement the 1%
> overweight position using futures.
> The additional cost of obtaining leverage for each option is as follows:
> ✓ ETF: (USD5 million × 0.6667 × 2.15%)/USD5 million = 1.43% (or 143 bps)
> ✓ Futures: (USD5 million × 0.6667 × 1.80%)/USD5 million = 1.20% (or 120 bps)
> where the inputs are derived as follows:
> ✓ 0.6667 reflects the three times leverage factor (66.67% borrowed and 33.33% cash
> usage).
> ✓ 2.15% reflects the ETF borrowing rate (three-month MRR of 1.80% + 35 bps).
> ✓ 1.80% reflects the absence of investment income offset (at three-month MRR) versus the
> unlevered cost of futures implementation.
> The total levered cost of each option is the sum of the unlevered cost plus the additional cost
> of obtaining leverage:
> ✓
> ETF: 27 bps + 143 bps = 170 bps
> ✓ Futures: 32 bps + 120 bps = 152 bps
> This 18-bps cost advantage would make futures the appropriate choice for the endowment’s
> investment team.

### 6. Discuss Smith’s method for estimating the increase in return expectations de rived from

increasing the endowment allocation to private equity.

> **答案要点**
>
> • Private equity is an illiquid alternative investment that can offer higher returns
> through a liquidity premium.
> • The illiquidity premium (or liquidity premium) is the expected compensation for the
> risk of tying up capital for an uncertain period.
> • It can be estimated, like Smith's method, using the concept that the discount for
> capital commitment is shown by the put option value with an exercise price equal to
> the illiquid asset's hypothetical “marketable price” at purchase time.
> Smith can get the price of the illiquid private equity asset by subtracting the put
> •
> price from the “marketable price.”
> • If both prices are estimated or known, the expected return for each can be
> calculated and the difference in expected returns is the illiquidity premium (in %).

### 7. Discuss two reasons the increased risk profile is appropriate. Justify your response.

> improve diversification, expected returns and access more strategies. Illiquid assets
> may shift portfolio's efficient frontier upward in mean–variance models for greater
> efficiency.
> • The endowment's portfolio risk profile is more conservative than that of peer
> universities.
> Smith's Monte Carlo simulations show the proposed asset allocation has a higher
> •
> chance of reaching the return target and better preserving the endowment's
> purchasing power.

### 8. Discuss the relevance of the endowment’s spending policy to Brodka’s expressed

concerns.

> **答案要点**
>
> • Brodka voices concerns that a higher allocation to illiquid investments may
> negatively affect the endowment's spending rate and risk profile.
> • Kemney University's spending policy follows a geometric smoothing rule (Yale
> formula), aiming for a predictable distribution pattern for better resource planning
> across various conditions.
> • The spending policy aligns with the investment objective of achieving long-term
> returns to support the spending rate and preserve endowment value over time.
> • The policy design has a smoothing, countercyclical element, resulting in lower
> spending rates with strong returns and higher spending rates in a weak return
> environment.

### 9. Discuss the actions that Grides should take to alleviate Brodka’s concerns.

> **答案要点**
>
> • Allocation changes lead to a reduction in liquid and semi-liquid categories and an
> increase in the illiquid category under normal and stress conditions, raising the
> overall illiquidity profile.
> • For Brodka's concern on liquidity profile, Grides must ensure proposed allocation
> complies with the liquidity budgeting framework under stress. From ongoing
> management, closely monitor and stress test portfolio's liquidity profile periodically
> to maintain adequacy.
> • Regarding Brodka's concern of risk profile “drift,” illiquid assets have high
> rebalancing costs. Adequate liquid assets and rebalancing mechanisms are vital in
> stress periods to keep risk profile within targets.
> • As liquid assets will decrease due to proposed allocation, Grides should adopt and
> follow an effective rebalancing mechanism before and after investment, like
> calendar or percent-range rebalancing, or an automatic method.
> • In severe market downturns, the design may exacerbate the endowment's liquidity
> needs, introducing undesired risks relevant to Kemney's long-term planning.

### 10. Clive Staples is a consultant with the Leedsford Organization (“Leedsford”), a boutique

investment consulting firm serving large endowments and private foundations. Leedsford consults on tactical asset allocation (TAA) program development and implementation, and on ongoing TAA idea generation. Staples has just completed his quarterly client review of the Narnea Foundation (“the Foundation”). Based on the Foundation’s current asset allocation and Leeds ford’s updated fair value models, Staples believes an exploitable TAA opportunity exists in US large-cap growth stocks. He recommends a 2% overweight position to the US equities policy allocation through either an unlevered ETF or total return swap exposures to the Russell 1000 Growth Index. Compare the efficiency of the ETF and total return swap TAA implementation alternatives from the perspectives of capital commitment, liquidity, and tracking error.

> **答案要点**
>
> Capital Commitment:
> • From a cash “usage” perspective, a Russell 1000 Growth ETF is less efficient (needs
> larger cash outlay) than a total return swap replicating the index.
> • An unlevered ETF’s capital commitment is the full notional value, while a total return
> swap offers similar exposure with much lower capital.
> • The cash-efficient nature of derivatives like total return swaps makes them useful
> for getting incremental exposure to an asset class.
> Liquidity:
> • From a liquidity perspective, a Russell 1000 Growth ETF is more efficient than the
> total return swap.
> ETFs are exchange-traded standardized products with liquid trading and narrow bid–
> •
> ask spreads.
> • Total return swaps are over-the-counter contracts (not exchange traded), negotiable
> and customizable regarding maturity, leverage and cost.
> Tracking Error:
> • From a tracking error perspective, ETFs are less efficient than the total return swap.
> • A Russell 1000 Growth ETF has tracking error due to premiums/discounts to NAV,
> cash drag or regulatory requirements.
> • Total return swaps offer exact replication; the Foundation gets the total index return
> without tracking error as the swap counterparty is obliged to provide it.
> • However, total return swaps expose the Foundation to counterparty credit risk and
> add complexities in managing net exposure during the contract.
